P2Poolv2 for Bitcoin Cash

Permissionless mining.
Continuously audited.
Self-custodial payouts.

P2Poolv2 is a Bitcoin Cash mining pool that its miners run. Their nodes keep one chain of shares between them, and each block the pool finds pays them in its coinbase.

Permissionless

The pool checks your work, not your identity. Run your own node, or declare your own template over Stratum V2, and you choose the transactions you mine.

Continuously audited

Every node keeps the share chain and recomputes the payout of every block, so every node checks that each miner is paid correctly.

Self-custodial payouts

Payouts arrive in the coinbase of each block the pool finds, on a Bitcoin Cash address you hold.

One share chain, checked by every node

A pool server keeps the books: it counts each miner's shares and decides what each block pays. P2Poolv2 moves those books into a chain of shares that every node keeps and checks, and pays miners straight from the block.

A chain of shares
Each node builds work from the templates of its own Bitcoin Cash Node (BCHN) and relays every share it finds to its peers. A share is a Bitcoin Cash block whose work meets the share chain's target. That target follows the share chain's own ASERT, which aims for a share every ten seconds. Two share chains run: main, and mini, whose target is 256 times easier for smaller miners.
A graph, not a line
A share may name up to three uncles: shares that lost a race to another share. The uncle's miner is paid nine tenths of its weight and the share that names it earns the other tenth, so work that arrives late is still paid. Each uncle is paid once.
PPLNS, paid from the coinbase
When a share also meets the Bitcoin Cash target, it is a block. Its coinbase pays the project donation (1% by default), any operator fee, and then the shares that hold the last three blocks' worth of work, at most 120,960 of them, leaving out the newest 100. Every node rebuilds that coinbase and rejects a share whose coinbase pays differently.
Every miner paid in the coinbase
The coinbase pays every miner its exact share directly, smallest first, up to Bitcoin Cash's own 1,000,000-byte transaction limit, about 29,400 payouts. A share that merge-mines tokens keeps its coinbase within 3,072 bytes, about 77 payouts. Anyone past the limit is paid through one output locked to a payout tree: keyless transactions that, once the coinbase matures, can only pay those miners their exact amounts less a fee of about one satoshi per byte. Every node broadcasts them.
Shares you can sell
A share's payout right can be sold for BCH through a hash time locked contract on each chain, the share chain and Bitcoin Cash, so a small miner can be paid now rather than when the pool next finds a block. The contracts are in the code and tested inside the node; they have not yet run through BCHN.

Who keeps the books

Stratum V2 Job Declaration lets a miner build its own template, and that is real progress. But a pool server that keeps the accounting decides what it pays a miner for. A pool operator is a company in a jurisdiction, and companies can be pressured. Such a pool does not need to reject a template; it only needs to stop counting the shares mined on it.

How to mine

Point your miners at a node: your own, or one you trust. Stratum V2 comes first. Stratum V1 is there for firmware that cannot speak V2.

Bring two addresses

Your payout address, a CashAddr such as bitcoincash:q..., receives your share of each block's coinbase. Your share address, such as p2pool:q..., owns your shares on the share chain; it is what you sell if you sell shares. Make it from a P2PKH address in your own wallet. The command takes no private key: 

$ p2poolv2_cli bch-share-address --network mainnet bitcoincash:q...
# p2pool:q...

The share address prefix is p2pool: on mainnet, tp2pool: on chipnet and rp2pool: on regtest. The two addresses are different things; never use one in place of the other.

Stratum V2

# the node's sv2_port; config.sample.toml suggests 3336
host           <node>:<sv2_port>
user identity  <payout CashAddr>.<worker>
               add ,p2p=<share address> when the node sets no miner_address
authority key  the node's public key, when it requires encryption

Standard and extended channels are supported, and so is Job Declaration in both modes, for a declarator that follows the Bitcoin Cash coinbase contract. Without an authority key the frames are not encrypted, so keep that port on localhost or a trusted network.

Stratum V1

url       stratum+tcp://<node>:3333
user      <payout CashAddr>.<worker>    # the bitcoincash: prefix is optional
password  x, or p2p=<share address> when the node sets no miner_address

When you are paid

Your payout is in the coinbase of each block the pool finds while your shares are in its window. It becomes spendable after 100 blocks, as for any Bitcoin Cash coinbase.

Run a node

A node of your own keeps you in control. It builds your templates from your BCHN, checks every share it hears, and serves your miners. The pool is only as decentralised as the number of nodes in it.

BCHN enforces Bitcoin Cash consensus and its own relay policy, including the adaptive block size limit. P2Poolv2 takes its templates and the block size limit from BCHN and adds no limit of its own: Bitcoin Cash can scale and will scale. 

# bitcoin.conf for BCHN
server=1
rpcuser=p2pool
rpcpassword=<your password>
zmqpubhashblock=tcp://127.0.0.1:28332

# config.toml, under [stratum]
chain = "bitcoin-cash"
bitcoin_cash_network = "mainnet"   # or "chipnet", "regtest"
bch_share_chain = "mini"           # "main" for large miners
miner_address = "p2pool:q..."
zmqpubhashblock = "tcp://127.0.0.1:28332"

# under [network], the share chain peers to join
dial_peers = ["/ip4/203.0.113.1/tcp/6884"]

$ p2poolv2 config.toml

Before you start

  • Download the archive for your platform and config.sample.toml from the releases page, and check it against its .sha256 file.
  • Point [bitcoinrpc] at BCHN's RPC port, and zmqpubhashblock at its ZMQ endpoint.
  • List peers in dial_peers. Bitcoin Cash has no share chain DNS seeds yet.
  • Try regtest or chipnet before mainnet.
  • The quickstart has every step.

Watch your node

Dashboard

Every node serves one at /dashboard on its API port: hashrate, the share chain, found blocks and each miner's part of the next payout. The node renders it in Rust and the pages run no script.

Where the code is today

P2Poolv2 for Bitcoin Cash is alpha software. It runs on chipnet, the Bitcoin Cash test network for upcoming upgrades, and is not yet a proven production pool. Try it on regtest or chipnet first.

Measured

  • 15 October 2026: two nodes on chipnet relayed and accepted each other's shares.
  • 18 October 2026: the first chipnet block, height 328,451, found by an Avalon ASIC over Stratum V1 through a two-node pool. Its coinbase paid the donation and the PPLNS window.
  • Both Job Declaration modes mined blocks on a two-node BCHN regtest over encrypted Stratum V2.
  • 18 October 2026: two CPU miners on regtest were paid in 38 coinbases, and every one of those outputs was later spent.

Not yet

  • Share trading: tested inside the node, not yet through BCHN or on chipnet.
  • Payout trees: executed on BCHN regtest, not yet on chipnet.
  • How many payouts stock ASIC firmware can mine has not yet been measured on Bitcoin Cash.
  • Stratum V2 and Job Declaration clients from other projects have not yet been tested against the pool.
  • The terminal UI is not yet in the release archives.

The plan carries each requirement with its evidence and date.
